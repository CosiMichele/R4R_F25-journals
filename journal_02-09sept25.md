## Rose, Bud, Thorn

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Due Wednesday 10th, 11pm**

### Rose

I found the discussion on data management plans especially relevant. It reminded me of a situation in my lab. About five years ago, a student built a tool that automatically collected and processed data in a very accessible way. The system continues to work well, but we recently discovered that new detector locations were added throughout Phoenix, but not included in the pipeline because no one documented the source of the original data. If there had been a clearer data management plan or documentation of where the data came from, fixing the problem would have been much easier. Instead, we had to track down contacts at ADOT, and even they were unsure of the original sources. This experience underscored for me how critical proper data documentation and management are.

### Thorn

I am still unsure how much responsibility graduate students should take on for tasks like building and maintaining data infrastructure. In my lab, much of this infrastructure was created years ago, and now staff have been hired to maintain it. On one hand, I want to be responsible for managing my data appropriately; on the other, we have IT staff whose role is to handle much of this work. In class, we discussed NSF’s requirement for data management plans, which I would be responsible for as a researcher seeking funding, but I’m unclear about how much of the actual content should come from me versus existing IT staff.

### Bud

Better documentation is something I know will save me significant time in the future. I often skip documentation in the moment, which comes back to haunt me when writing journal papers and reviewers ask for revisions that I can’t easily trace in my models. Even if this seems more like a modeling issue than a data one, I think they are closely connected. Moving forward, I plan to be more intentional about recording metadata, creating SOPs, and building documentation that will also help future students who take over my projects.
