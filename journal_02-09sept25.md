### Rose
Data management is highly applicable to my research. The concept of the data life cycle and the use of a Data Management Plan (DMP) are directly relevant, as they provide structured guidance on handling data before, during, and after a project. In my Long COVID research, we often use a Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) for quantitative studies or a review protocol for systematic and scoping reviews (qualitative studies). While these tools guide analysis and methodological rigor, a DMP uniquely addresses how data are collected, organized, documented, preserved, and shared, ensuring that they align with FAIR and CARE principles. Applying a DMP helps ensure data are well-managed in the present, responsibly preserved for the future, ethically handled with respect to participants, and potentially reusable for collective scientific benefit.

### Thorn
One area I find challenging is applying the FAIR and CARE guidelines to making datasets fully Findable and Accessible in my research. Ethical concerns in public health and clinical research often limit how openly data can be shared, even when de-identified. I believe there is a need for clearer guidance on how FAIR principles apply to sensitive health data and under what circumstances de-identified datasets should be shared to advance research while still upholding ethical responsibilities.

### Bud
One aspect that could be applied to my work is stronger advocacy for the use of Data Management Plans (DMPs) in public health research. While quantitative studies often emphasize Statistical Analysis Plans (SAPs), DMPs are equally critical, as they shape the efficiency and integrity of a project. Incorporating training in data management practices, including the use of computational tools such as GitHub, REDCap, and Qualtrics, as well as proper labeling and documentation into introductory research courses would be valuable. These practices should not be assumed as common knowledge but explicitly taught, as they are essential for ensuring well-structured, reproducible, and ethically sound research.
