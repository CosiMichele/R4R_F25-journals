### Rose

Some of the pillars of open science that are already working in my research are: open-access publications, open-source software, open-data, and open methodology. Thanks to open-access data available from many sources, I can access climate data in climate change projection analysis of projected extremes, as well as access observational data to provide context for my analysis. Additionally, other sources allow me to easily access the data needed to generate weather simulations. Open-source software and open methodology are what have allowed me to analyze, and visualize the raw and processed data. Finally, open-access publications allow me to understand the context of my research, compare results with other studies, incorporate new ideas into the analysis, and effectively share my results.

## Research Application

Applying open science to my research topic in hydroclimate extremes can initially be done with three concepts:
1.	Making raw and processed data freely available in digital repositories, adhering to the FAIR guiding principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable). This ensures data accessibility for validation and collaboration.
2.	Informational and educational materials can be created and shared as open resources. Creating visualization products, such as maps or animations, helps present complex data in a more understandable and accessible way. Perhaps, including methodologies and code for these visualizations (ideally also being open) can be included.
3.	Publish research papers detailing findings on hydroclimate extremes in open-access journals or repositories, allowing free access to critical information for a broad audience.
