### Rose
What worked and is directly applicable to my research during the FOSS session was learning how to design clear, structured prompts using the CRAFT framework (Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone). This will help me frame precise questions when summarizing or synthesizing Long COVID literature. Prompt chaining also stood out as useful for breaking down complex tasks, for example, first extracting study characteristics, then comparing findings, and finally identifying research gaps. In addition, the ability to upload documents for analysis is highly relevant for reviewing multiple Long COVID studies at once, while custom instructions can help ensure output remains evidence-based and consistent. Finally, the session’s emphasis on recognizing and mitigating pitfalls like hallucinations and vague outputs is especially important for applying LLMs responsibly in sensitive research areas such as Long COVID.

### Thorn
What I didn’t understand as well, and what I think would be more challenging to apply to my research, is the use of Model Context Protocols (MCPs) and other advanced integrations that allow LLMs to directly interact with files, run code, or connect with external tools. While this could be powerful for streamlining data analysis in Long COVID research, I am still unclear about the technical setup, security concerns, and how to ensure reproducibility when models execute commands or manipulate datasets. I also anticipate challenges in managing token limits when uploading large volumes of literature or datasets, which may restrict the depth of analysis the LLM can perform. In addition, I am concerned about how my data will be protected, since I work with sensitive information involving human subjects, and ensuring privacy and compliance with ethical standards is critical.

### Bud
What could potentially be applied to my work includes using structured prompts through the CRAFT framework to generate clearer, more targeted outputs when synthesizing Long COVID literature or drafting manuscripts. Prompt chaining could help manage complex analyses by breaking tasks into steps, such as summarizing studies, identifying patterns, and then highlighting gaps. Few-shot prompting is also applicable, as I could provide examples of high-quality summaries or tables to guide the model in producing consistent results. Beyond my own research, I believe these methods of communicating with LLMs should be taught in basic classes as AI becomes more widespread, so that students learn how best to use AI responsibly and avoid misuse. It is not sufficient to simply discourage AI use in research; since LLMs are here to stay, the most effective approach is to teach students how to apply them thoughtfully and correctly.
