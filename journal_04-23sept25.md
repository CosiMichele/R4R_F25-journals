### Rose

This reading gave me new perspective on how AI / LLMs (large language models) can be tools even in course work, not just in high-level research. Although I’m not doing independent research yet, I often write lab reports, process data, and draft explanations, and I can see how LLMs could help me accelerate those tasks if used carefully.
For example:
When writing a lab report, I could ask an LLM to help structure the sections, suggest transitions, or polish phrasing.
If I’m stuck describing a technical method (e.g. an experimental protocol), I could prompt the LLM with the steps and ask it to produce a concise description I can refine.
For data or results interpretation, I might give it a small summary (e.g. “Here’s mean, standard deviation, sample counts …”) and ask it to suggest possible interpretations or flags to check (e.g. outliers, anomalies) — then I critically examine its suggestions.
That said, I appreciated how the reading emphasizes that LLMs don’t really understand, and they may hallucinate or provide misleading information. So the human oversight is crucial.

### Bud

Here are some ideas I want to try:
Craft a “help me edit” prompt for my next lab report:
e.g. “Act as a scientific editor. Here’s my draft introduction + methods. Suggest improvements to clarity, consistency, and conciseness, and point out ambiguous phrases.”
Then compare its suggestions to what I or the TA would have done, so I learn prompts and editing.
Use few-shot prompting for method descriptions: when I have to write a procedure, I’ll give two or three example methods and then ask the LLM to generate the next with similar style. This helps me see how giving examples helps direct output.
Prompt auditing checklist: after getting an answer, I’ll reflexively ask:
“What assumptions did the LLM make?”
“Are any statements unsupported or vague?”
“Can I cross-check any claims via textbooks or primary sources?”
Over time, these habits will help me use LLMs more responsibly and effectively.

## Research Application

Even though I’m not in full research mode, this reading gives me ideas to improve how I do my coursework now:
Writing / Editing Reports: Instead of staring at a blank page, I can prompt an LLM to generate an outline or help me reorganize a messy draft. I’ll always critically review the output.
Explaining Methods / Interpretation: If I run an experiment and get results, I can feed a short summary to the LLM and ask, “What patterns or concerns should I check?” — then I use that as a brainstorming partner (not as truth).
Prompt Reuse & Templates: As I write multiple lab reports or assignments, I can develop prompt templates (e.g. “You are my lab mentor; critique this methods section under 300 words.”). That way I reuse what works.
Learning by example: By examining the LLM’s outputs and its mistakes, I’ll deepen my own understanding of how to phrase explanations, spot errors, and guide logic.
Document + prompt integration: In future, if labs require working with multiple files (data, code, notes), I might experiment with uploading or pasting file snippets and asking the LLM to work across them (e.g. “Using this data snippet and this method, check for consistency between method write-up and the results table”).
